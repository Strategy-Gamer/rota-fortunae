# Rota Fortunae — Economy design (canonical, from the design doc)

Source: `Rota Fortunae_ Civilizations.txt` (economy section ~paras 880–1046). This file is the **permanent**
record of the intended economic model so it does not have to be re-derived from chat context. If the doc
and this file disagree, the doc wins — update this file.

> **Model rewritten (Increment 17):** the earlier **district** model (Stellaris-like District/Tier chains,
> a 4th *Urbanite* pop, Materials + a goods/plantation split, worker-owned *Subsistence*) has been
> **retired** to `economy_old.py` and replaced by the simpler, higher-detail **land** model below:
> **3 pops, 3 land states, 3 goods, all land elite-owned.**

## The one thing to never get wrong: there is NO GDP

The model has **no GDP anywhere**. Everywhere a "GDP per capita" instinct appears, use **Wealth per
capita** instead. Two hard rules:

1. **Wealth is never negative.** Wealth is a value *generated by land* (surplus / exchange value). A pop's
   *share* of wealth can be **zero** (no excess), but it cannot go below zero.
2. **Below subsistence = lacking food, not negative wealth.** Food is a *good*, not money. When a pop can't
   meet its needs, that shows up as a **food shortage** (→ famine → disease/mortality), handled on the goods
   side — wealth simply sits at (or near) zero.

**Consumption is free.** Food / Consumables do **not** cost pops money to consume. Instead, the *amount* a
pop consumes scales with its **wealth per capita**. So wealth per capita is the master dial for standard of
living; the goods just have to physically exist to be consumed.

## Pops (3 types)

A pop = a group with a `type` and `civilization`; everything else (literacy, religion %, etc.) is a
percentage of the pop, not a split.

**Standard of Living (SoL)** is determined by, in order of importance: **Wealth per capita (mainly)**,
Food access, Goods (consumables) access, Security. **SoL drives:** population growth, unrest, migration,
class mobility, treasury contribution (partly).

- **Slaves** — work jobs for free; assigned **before** commoners; receive only subsistence living; earn
  **no wages** (their whole labor share of wealth accrues to the owner).
- **Commoners** — work land for wages (rural and urban alike, now that Urbanite is gone).
- **Elites** — do **not** work; **own all land** and receive its surplus; generate **elite pressure** when
  their numbers exceed **elite opportunities**.

## Goods (3)

- **Food** — grown on Cultivated & raised on Pastoral land. The baseline for **carrying capacity**. Food
  demand scales with population; demand > supply → population stress → famine.
- **Consumables** — general urban manufactured goods, produced on Urban land. Demand scales with **wealth
  per capita** (richer pops demand more). *(Deferred: goods balance feeding back into urban wealth output &
  wealthy-pop satisfaction.)*
- **Wealth** — value generated by all productive activity (GDP-like), the **tax base**; cannot be traded
  directly. Distributed between commoners (wages) and elites (surplus) by the **wage share**. Slaves earn
  none. Can be consumed or invested (Rural/Urban Capital). See the two hard rules above.

## Land (3 states; per location — replaces districts)

Each location's arable land is divided into three states. **All land is elite-owned** (simplification).

- **Pastoral** — the **default** land (unused/destroyed land reverts to it). Small food + wealth surplus,
  **no capital upkeep**, cheap, low elite opportunity, least vulnerable.
- **Cultivated** — **large food + moderate wealth**; has **capital upkeep**; created out of pastoral by
  investing wealth; vulnerable to low security / raids.
- **Urban** — **lots of wealth + Consumables, NO food**; heavy capital upkeep; most vulnerable; most elite
  opportunity; needs Town+ tier *(tier gating deferred)*.

**Capital** (Rural boosts Cultivated/Pastoral & maintains Cultivated; Urban boosts/maintains Urban) is a
**stub multiplier on output** this pass — real invest → upkeep → depreciation → security-attrition is
deferred to its own increment.

## Labor & Production

Land + capital set the **job capacity**; workers are pulled onto land weighted by pay (`job_pull`).
**Output is a nonlinear (saturating) scale** — more workers than jobs still yields positive marginal output
(no hard cliff). **Slaves are assigned first**; commoners take the labor pool left after them.

**Wealth distribution:** per parcel, `wage_share` (falls as workers crowd the jobs) splits wealth into a
labor share vs elite surplus. The commoner share of the labor pool = `commoners / (slaves + commoners)`
(slaves' share is confiscated by the owner). Elite gets the surplus + the confiscated slave share.

## Elite Opportunities

`elite opportunities = Σ (per-land-type opportunities) + accessible state opportunities`. Local
opportunities come from each land type (Urban > Cultivated > Pastoral); the **state pool** (patronage /
bureaucracy / military) is a **stub arg** for now. Elite pressure / overproduction = elites in **excess** of
opportunities.

## Security & Control (two different gates)

- **Security** — "can people safely live, invest, farm, trade, store wealth here?" Sets the investment time
  horizon; gates the organized economy; low security burns fixed capital & converts land toward pastoral.
- **Control** — "can the state impose its will here?" Enables taxation, conscription, policy enforcement;
  low control → autonomy / separatism / breakaway.

## Object model in code (`economy.py`) — OOP scaffolding for the DoD C++ port

Written OOP for now (clarity/experimentation); the shipping engine is DoD in C++, so keep the *data* on
these objects clean and *behaviour* in plain methods a DoD port can turn into systems-over-arrays. Shared
helper math lives in `extra_functions.py` (`get_output`, `get_wage_share`, `get_job_pull`, wealth ladder,
shortage modifier) and is **reused, not re-implemented**.

- **`LandType`** — the archetype of a land state (no tiers): per-worker `outputs{food|consumables|wealth}`,
  `labor_per_area`, `land_per_area`, `saturation`, `exp`, `elite_opps_per_area`, `security_exposure`,
  `capital_weight`, `produces_food`. Concrete: **`PASTORAL`** `{food 1.2, wealth 0.3}`, **`CULTIVATED`**
  `{food 2.5, wealth 1.0}`, **`URBAN`** `{wealth 3.0, consumables 2.0}` (no food). In `LAND_TYPES`.
- **`Parcel`** — a quantity of one land type in a location, **always elite-owned**: `land_type, area,
  capital (stub mult), owner (Elite Pop), workers`. Methods: `job_capacity`, `land_use`,
  `elite_opportunities`, `wage_share` (worker_owned_count = 0 now), `job_pull`, `produce` (→{good:amount}).
- **`Pop`** — group of one `pop_type` (Slave/Commoner/Elite) & `civ`, with `amount` and `wealth`. **Pop
  wealth is a MONTHLY RUNNING TOTAL, not an accumulating stock** — overwritten each tick with this month's
  income (`set_wealth`, clamped ≥ 0). `wealth_level()` uses the log wealth ladder; `food_need`/`goods_need`
  scale with wealth level; `standard_of_living()` = 0.55·wealth_level + 0.25·food + 0.10·goods + 0.10·sec.
- **`Location`** — thin harness tying land + pops for one `tick()`: allocate rural labor (Slaves+Commoners,
  slaves first) across parcels by pay-weighted pull, produce food/consumables/wealth, distribute (commoner
  wages vs elite surplus, slaves unpaid), set food/goods access & security on pops. Returns `elite_income,
  commoner_income, loc_wage_share, output, food, consumables, wealth`. `python economy.py` runs a smoke test.

## How this maps onto the abstract secular-cycle sim (`new_model.py`, FinalSim)

The sim is a **single abstracted location**. It creates one elite-owned **CULTIVATED** parcel of area
`econ_land` (≈ the old lone FARM) + a Commoner pop + an Elite pop, and each tick: sets commoner amount &
security, calls `location.tick()`, and reads **commoner wage** (relative-wage `w` → mass mobilization),
**commoner `food_access`** (tracked; famine mortality deferred), **elite surplus** (`elite_income` → the
fiscal block), and **`elite_opportunities()`** (baseline elite positions). Wealth per capita is clamped ≥ 0;
crowding that would drive it "negative" shows up as a food shortage instead. Increments 1–16 built the
secular-cycle dynamics on top; swapping districts→land (Increment 17) preserved them (ordering P→E→U,
≈40/20/40 split, E resets each cycle, robust) since the sim reads ratios, not absolute output scale.
