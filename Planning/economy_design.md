# Rota Fortunae — Economy design (canonical, from the design doc)

Source: `Rota Fortunae_ Civilizations.docx` (economy at paras ~372–589; data structures ~70–101;
extracted to `_docx_dump.txt`). This file is the **permanent** record of the intended economic model
so it does not have to be re-derived from chat context. If the doc and this file disagree, the doc wins —
update this file.

## The one thing to never get wrong: there is NO GDP

The model has **no GDP anywhere**. Everywhere a "GDP per capita" instinct appears, use **Wealth per
capita** instead. Two hard rules:

1. **Wealth is never negative.** Wealth is a value *generated by districts* (surplus / exchange value).
   A pop's *share* of wealth can be **zero** (no excess), but it cannot go below zero.
2. **Below subsistence = lacking food, not negative wealth.** Food is a *good*, not money. When a pop
   can't meet its needs, that shows up as a **food shortage** (→ famine → disease/mortality), handled on
   the goods side — wealth simply sits at (or near) zero.

**Consumption is free.** Food / Materials / Goods do **not** cost pops money to consume. Instead, the
*amount* a pop consumes scales with its **wealth per capita**. So wealth per capita is the master dial
for standard of living; the goods just have to physically exist to be consumed.

## Pops

A pop = a group with a `type` and `civilization`; everything else (literacy, religion %, etc.) is a
percentage of the pop, not a split. Size 1 … 2³².

**Standard of Living (SoL)** is determined by, in order of importance:
- **Wealth per capita (mainly)**
- Food access
- Goods access
- Security

**SoL drives:** population growth, unrest, migration, class mobility, treasury contribution (partly).

Types:
- **Slaves** — work rural jobs for free; *all* wealth surplus goes to owners; receive only subsistence;
  assigned to labor *before* commoners.
- **Commoners** — can work rural districts for wages; otherwise self-employed on unused arable/pastoral
  capacity (keep their own output).
- **Urbanites** — work urban districts for wages; important for innovation, legacy, administration.
- **Elites** — do **not** work; **own all districts** and receive their surplus; generate **elite
  pressure** when their numbers exceed **elite opportunities**.

## Goods (the real, physical outputs)

- **Food** — grown in Arable & Pastoral districts. Survival/biological constraint. Famine → increased
  disease risk.
- **Materials** — raw extractive inputs.
- **Goods** — have types; variety + sufficient quantity raise satisfaction & SoL.
- **Wealth** — a *value signal, not a normally-traded good*. Represents surplus/exchange value; is the
  **tax base**. (This is the money-like quantity — but see the two hard rules above.)

## Land (three states; per location)

- **Arable** — used by Arable Agriculture districts & self-employed rural pops. Good Food & Wealth.
  Vulnerable to raids/low security. Upgrades from pastoral (investment; more on hard terrain).
- **Pastoral** — used by Pastoral districts & self-employed pops. Small wealth & food, plus nomadic
  cavalry. Cheap & less vulnerable.
- **Unused** — wilderness / abandoned farmland / unsafe countryside. **Low security converts
  arable/pastoral → unused.** Recovery restores unused → *pastoral first*.

Arable & Pastoral **capacity** both compete for the same land area (arable use removes pastoral
capacity). **Unused capacity is free for commoners** (self-employed subsistence). Terrain/vegetation/
climate set the caps and the investment needed — terrain determines *what is possible*, not just ±%
modifiers.

## Labor

- **Rural labor** — from Slaves & Commoners; used by rural districts & subsistence (Farm, Pasture,
  Plantation, Extraction, Infrastructure). **Slaves assigned first.**
- **Urban labor** — from Urbanites; used by urban districts (Industry, Commerce, Administration,
  Culture, Education, Religion, Military).

## Districts (the scale-providers)

"Locations determine what is possible. Pops at what scale. **Districts provide the scale.**"
A district has: Type, Tier, Labor Demand, Input Demand, Output, **Elite Opportunities**, Security
Exposure, other effects. Owners (elites) must **build/upgrade** them (Stellaris-like tier chains, e.g.
Farm → Intensive → Mechanized → Precision; Workshops → Manufactories → Factories → Production Plants).
Rural types: Farm, Pasture, Plantation, Mining, Fortifications, Infrastructure. Urban types:
Administration, Commercial, Culture, Industrial, Education, Religion, Military. **Resource Nodes**
(tech-gated) raise the max district cap & output.

**Elite Opportunities** = the number of district (+ state) slots elites can occupy. Elite pressure /
overproduction = elites in **excess** of opportunities. Elites relieve overproduction by **building more
districts** (when they have surplus and security allows).

## Security & Control (two different gates)

- **Security** — "can people safely live, invest, farm, trade, store wealth here?" Sets the **investment
  time horizon** (90–100% industrial … 0–30% depopulation). Limiting factor in development. Raids/warfare
  lower it; forts/terrain/local armies modify exposure. Low security burns sedentary fixed capital
  (years of value); nomads barely care.
- **Control** — "can the state impose its will here?" Enables **taxation, conscription, policy
  enforcement**. Low control → autonomy/separatism/breakaway.

## Country / State layer

- **State Revenue = Σ(local wealth × effective tax rate × control/capacity modifiers).** The state owns
  nothing — it **only taxes** wealth.
- Tracks: State Expenses, Balance, Treasury, **State Capacity** (admin+extraction ability), Elite
  Opportunities, Elite Satisfaction, Goods Balance, Resilience, Unrest Threshold, Legitimacy.

## The secular cycle these produce (doc's own phase notes)

- Expansion: few but growing Commoners, few but growing Elites.
- Stagnation: many & stagnant Commoners, many & growing Elites — **elites enjoy a golden age**.
- Crisis: many but shrinking Commoners, many but shrinking Elites.
- Depression: few & stagnant Commoners, many but shrinking Elites — **strong elites can lengthen the
  depression** (e.g. Mamluks).

## How this maps onto the abstract secular-cycle sim (`new_model.py`, FinalSim)

The sim is a **single abstracted location**. The mapping:
- **Wealth per capita** replaces every "GDP per capita"; it is the main SoL driver → births, unrest
  (low wealth → high mass-mobilization), class mobility (low commoner wealth vs elite → climb).
- **Food** is a *separate* track: food output vs need → food shortage → famine severity → (later)
  mortality via `get_death_rate`. Never routed through wealth.
- Wealth per capita is **clamped ≥ 0**; the crowding that would drive it "negative" instead shows up as
  a **food shortage**.
- Elites take **district wealth surplus**; commoners get **wages (a share of district wealth) + their
  self-employed subsistence-plot wealth**.
- Security gates the organized (district) economy; subsistence always remains as a floor.
- Deferred wiring (kept as tracked read-outs until their increment): district wealth → elite income &
  state fiscal base; districts → carrying capacity; food shortage → famine/disease mortality; goods →
  satisfaction/SoL.

## Object model in code (`economy.py`) — OOP scaffolding for the DoD C++ port

Written OOP for now (clarity/experimentation); the shipping engine is DoD in C++, so keep the *data* on
these objects clean and *behaviour* in plain methods a DoD port can turn into systems-over-arrays.
Shared helper math lives in `extra_functions.py` (wealth levels, `get_output`, `get_wage_share`,
`get_job_pull`, `get_land_productivity`, shortage modifier) and is reused, not re-implemented.

- **`Tier`** — a district **sub-type**: its own per-worker `outputs{good:base}` AND `inputs{good:base}`
  (+ optional `labor_factor`). Tiers are NOT just an output multiplier — higher tiers shift the mix and
  can require inputs (e.g. Basic Farming = bare wealth + subsistence food; Intensive = much more food +
  a ton more wealth; Mechanized = adds a `materials` input).
- **`DistrictType`** — shared definition/archetype: `name, category, tiers[Tier,...], labor_per_size,
  land_per_size, saturation, exp, elite_opps_per_size, security_exposure, worker_type, worker_owned`.
  Districts are **Stellaris-like**: SIZE scales jobs/output/land; TIER selects the active sub-type.
- **`District`** — one building instance: `dtype, size, tier, owner (Elite Pop | None), workers`.
  Methods: `job_capacity`, `land_use`, `elite_opportunities`, `wage_share`, `job_pull`, `produce`
  (→{good:amount}), `input_demand` (→{good:amount consumed}), `expand(size)`, `upgrade_tier()`.
- **Concrete types** (per the tier chains above): `FARM` (arable, food+wealth), `PASTURE` (pastoral,
  more land/output, +cavalry hook), `PLANTATION` (goods+wealth). **`SUBSISTENCE`** is a **hardcoded
  worker-owned** type (not an elite district): commoners self-employ unused capacity, `wage_share`=1.0,
  keep their output. Registered in `DISTRICT_TYPES`.
- **`Pop`** — group of one `pop_type` (Slave/Commoner/Urbanite/Elite) & `civ`, with `amount` and
  `wealth`. **Pop wealth is a MONTHLY RUNNING TOTAL, not an accumulating stock** — it is overwritten
  each tick with this month's income (`set_wealth`, clamped ≥ 0). Only treasuries (state, civ) and elite
  investment projects accumulate. `wealth_level()` uses the log wealth ladder; `food_need`/`goods_need`
  **scale with wealth level** (richer pops consume more goods); `standard_of_living()` = 0.55·wealth_level
  + 0.25·food + 0.10·goods + 0.10·security.
- **`Location`** — thin harness (not final design) that ties land + pops + districts for one `tick()`:
  elite districts consume land, subsistence fills the remainder, labor splits by pay-weighted job pull,
  districts produce, wealth distributes (wages→commoners, surplus→elite owners), food/goods access set.
- Not yet wired into FinalSim's cycle — this is the object substrate the elite-income increment will
  connect to. `python economy.py` runs a smoke-test demo.
